> [!STATUS] Direction, not commitment
> This chapter describes where Ovia Labs would like the work to lead. It does not commit to products, timelines or a network.

## A web where permissions are legible

In the long run, users should be able to answer three questions about their wallet at any moment, without specialist knowledge: **who can move my assets, how much, and why did I allow it?** Today the answers exist on-chain but are hard to reach. Ovia Protect is a first step towards making them ordinary to see.

## From individual checks to shared evidence

Some answers can be read directly from the chain. Others, such as which application a contract belongs to, rely on someone’s judgement. The long-term idea behind Ovia Network is that such judgements should be attributable, comparable and open to challenge, rather than hidden inside any single provider’s database.

## Principles that stay fixed

Plans will change as the work progresses. These principles are intended not to:

1. **Explain before alarming.** Information should help people decide, not frighten them into acting.
2. **Never claim certainty that does not exist.** No “safe” verdicts, no implied audits, no implied partners.
3. **Verify what can be verified.** On-chain facts are read from the chain and shown with their block.
4. **Collect as little as possible.** User data that is not collected cannot leak.
5. **Publish limitations with features.** A tool that hides its gaps is not a security tool.

## What success would look like

Success would be modest and concrete: people checking their approvals as routinely as they check a balance, fewer losses from forgotten allowances, and security information that anyone can trace back to its evidence. Whether Ovia Labs builds every part of that or contributes one part of it is less important than the direction.
