# The Problem

Every day, millions of digital transactions take place between people who have never met.

A freelancer accepts a project.

A customer purchases a digital product.

A company hires a contractor.

An investor enters an agreement.

In nearly every case, one question remains unanswered:

> **Who should act first?**

## The Trust Problem

Traditional transactions force one participant to make the first move.

The buyer sends payment before receiving the product.

The seller delivers the product before receiving payment.

Neither option is ideal.

If either participant fails to fulfill their obligation, the agreement breaks down.

This challenge exists across countless industries, regardless of whether value is exchanged as fiat, cryptocurrency, digital assets, services, or intellectual property.

This is known as **counterparty risk**.

---

## Existing Solutions

Today's solutions generally fall into two categories.

### Trust The Other Party

One participant simply hopes the other fulfills the agreement.

This works until it doesn't.

When trust fails, disputes begin.

### Trust A Third Party

Instead of trusting each other, both participants trust an intermediary.

Examples include:

* Marketplaces
* Payment processors
* Escrow companies
* Centralized platforms
* Arbitration services

These services reduce risk, but they also introduce new dependencies.

Someone else controls the process.

Someone else settles disputes.

Someone else decides the outcome.

Trust has simply shifted from one participant to another organization.

---

## Communication Was Solved

The internet transformed communication.

Information can move across the world almost instantly.

Messages.

Documents.

Images.

Video.

Communication is no longer the problem.

Trust still is.

---

## Ownership Was Solved

Blockchain technology introduced programmable ownership.

For the first time, users could own and transfer digital assets without relying on centralized institutions.

Ownership became decentralized.

Settlement became transparent.

Yet agreements between people still depended on trust.

Blockchain solved ownership.

It did not solve coordination.

## A Different Approach

Ovia Protocol approaches the problem differently.

Instead of asking participants to trust one another, the protocol asks both participants to trust the rules they agreed to before the transaction begins.

Those rules become transparent.

They become programmable.

They become enforceable.

No participant can unilaterally change the outcome after funds have been committed.

The protocol simply executes the agreement.

---

## The Goal

Ovia Protocol is not designed to replace communication.

It is not designed to replace ownership.

Its purpose is to provide the missing trust layer between those two systems.

Communication enables coordination.

Blockchain enables ownership.

**Ovia Protocol enables trustless execution.**

That is the problem the protocol was built to solve.
